Concept checks

Study10 min

The four core conditionals

TypeIf-clauseResult clauseUse it forExample
Zeropresent simplepresent simplefacts, rules, things that are always trueIf you mix blue and yellow, you get green.
Firstpresent simplewill / imperative / can + basereal, likely situations in the future; warnings, promisesIf it rains, we'll move inside.
Secondpast simple (were for all persons)would / could / might + baseimaginary or unlikely situations now or in the future; adviceIf I had more time, I'd learn the guitar.
Thirdpast perfectwould / could / might have + past participleimaginary past: regret, criticism, speculationIf we'd left earlier, we wouldn't have missed the train.
  • No will or would in the if-clause: If it will rain → If it rains. If I would have known → If I had known.
  • Comma rule: use a comma when the if-clause comes first. No comma is needed when it comes second.
  • Formal were: If I were you… is the standard advice form. If I was is common in casual speech but avoid it in writing.

Read the grammar box above. Then answer the question about each sentence. Think about what is really true.

  1. If I had a car, I'd drive to work.
    Do I have a car?
  2. If it's sunny tomorrow, we'll go hiking.
    Is hiking possible tomorrow?
  3. If she hadn't missed the bus, she would have arrived on time.
    Did she arrive on time?
  4. If you mix blue and yellow, you get green.
    Is this true every time?
  5. I wouldn't have called if I'd known you were busy.
    Did I know you were busy?
  6. If Tom spoke Spanish, he'd apply for the Madrid job.
    Is Tom applying for the Madrid job?
  7. Provided that the client agrees, we'll sign on Friday.
    Is the Friday signing certain?
  8. If the meeting had been canceled, someone would have emailed us.
    Does the speaker think the meeting was canceled?

Read the fine print

Study11 min

Alternatives to if

WordMeaningExample
unlessif … notUnless you cancel, your plan renews. (= If you don't cancel…)
as long as / provided (that) / providing (that)only if; this condition is necessaryYou can borrow my laptop as long as you return it tonight.
even ifthe condition does not change the resultI'll finish the report even if it takes all night.
in case B2+as a precaution; not a conditionTake an umbrella in case it rains. (Take it now, rain or no rain.)
  • Don't double the negative: Unless you don't hurry → Unless you hurry / If you don't hurry.
  • Like if, these words take a present verb for the future: as long as you will finish → as long as you finish.

Third conditional: three jobs

Regret (about yourself)
If I'd set a second alarm, I wouldn't have overslept.
Criticism (of someone else)
If you'd checked the address, we wouldn't have gotten lost.
Speculation (what was possible)

Use could have or might have instead of would have when the result was only possible, not certain. B2+ If it hadn't been for + noun = without: If it hadn't been for her help, I would have failed.

  1. The battery died → her alarm didn't go off.
  2. Her alarm didn't go off → she overslept.

  5. She arrived late → she didn't get the job.

Mixed conditionals: when the times don't match

DirectionFormExampleReal situation
Past condition → present resultIf + past perfect, would + baseIf I had taken that job, I would be living in Toronto now.I didn't take it. I live here now.
Present condition → past resultIf + past simple, would have + past participleIf I were more organized, I wouldn't have missed the deadline yesterday.I'm not organized (in general). I missed it.

Ask two questions: When is the condition? When is the result? If the answers are different, you need a mixed conditional. Time words (now, today, yesterday, last year) are your clues.

Opinion · Guest column
Car-Free Sundays: Why Our City Should Try It

Every city planner knows a simple rule: [1] if you give people more space to walk, they walk more. So why do our downtown streets still belong to cars every Sunday?

Last year, our neighbors in Riverton closed their main avenue to traffic one Sunday a month. The results surprised even the critics. Local cafes reported higher sales, and air quality improved noticeably. [2] If the city council had listened to the early complaints, the experiment would never have started. [3] And if it had never started, Riverton wouldn't be the lively, walkable town it is today.

Our own council has rejected similar proposals three times. Part of the problem is attitude: [4] if our leaders weren't so afraid of angry drivers, they would have approved the plan years ago.

Critics say business will suffer. The evidence suggests the opposite. [5] If we close Main Street for just one Sunday in June, shop owners will see the difference for themselves. Of course, the details matter: [6] unless buses keep running on the side streets, the plan will fail.

Imagine a Sunday morning with children cycling safely down the middle of Main Street. [7] If our city tried this, I think most residents would never want to go back.

[8] Should the council approve a trial, I will be the first to volunteer at the street fair. [9] Had they acted five years ago, they would have saved the city a lot of money. It is not too late to start.

Formal makeover

Study8 min

Inverted conditionals: formal style without if

ReplacesInverted formExample
First: If you need…Should + subject + base verbShould you need assistance, please call us.
Second: If I were to… / If I were…Were + subject + to + base / + adjective, nounWere I to resign, I would give notice. Were I in your position, I'd wait.
Third: If we had known…Had + subject + past participleHad we known, we would have called.
  • Negatives go after the subject, never contracted: Had I not seen it… (Hadn't I seen it). Were it not for your help…
  • Should + base verb: Should the alarm sound (sounds).
  • Inversion is for formal writing: notices, contracts, business email, academic text.

The Lakeview Hotel's guest notice sounds too casual. Rewrite each sentence with an inverted conditional (no if). Keep the meaning the same.

  1. If you need extra towels, please contact the front desk.
  2. If you require a late checkout, please ask at least 24 hours in advance.
  3. If the fire alarm sounds, leave the building by the nearest exit.
  4. If we had known about the elevator problem earlier, we would have warned guests.
  5. If the hotel were to close the pool for repairs, guests would receive a discount.
  6. B2+ If it were not for our dedicated staff, this hotel would not be the success it is today.
  7. B2+ If the storm had not damaged the roof, the spa would be open today.
